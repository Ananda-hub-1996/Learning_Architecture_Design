The Winnr Path: Employee Learning & Capability Development

From discovering the business to delivering meaningful work. A programme proposal that takes employees from understanding the organisation to proving they can perform in it.

The assignment

SmartWinnr wanted a structured curriculum for in-house employees that supports onboarding, product knowledge, functional competency and continuous professional development. Its functions are Sales, Client Engagement, Customer Success, Implementation, Product, Technology and Support.

The brief asked

1. How would you identify training needs and skill gaps?

My response: a five-step, business-aligned needs analysis that separates learning gaps from non-learning barriers, piloted in two functions before scaling.

Part 3, from page 25

The idea behind the proposal. The Winnr Path is more than an onboarding checklist. It is a journey from understanding the business, to understanding your responsibilities, to demonstrating your capabilities, to applying them at work. Progression depends on demonstrated competence, not course completion or time spent in training.

One architecture, many roles

The same four stages support all seven functions, with differentiation by role and responsibility.

Evidence of readiness

Readiness is shown through application and workplace evidence, not merely course completion.

Scalable by design

Shared foundations, reusable resources and prioritised pathways, not a separate curriculum for every employee.

The Winnr Path in detail

A common foundation for everyone, then learning that becomes progressively more specific to the function and the role.

DISCOVER

Business Ready

Common to all employees. Five learning areas:

  • Who we are: the business
  • Who we serve: customers and markets
  • What we offer: products and solutions
  • Why it matters: customer value
  • How we work together: culture

DECODE

Knowledge Ready

Four learning dimensions, applied by function:

  • Functional context
  • Role clarity
  • Functional mastery
  • Cross-functional collaboration

DEPLOY

Application Ready

Three components, matched to role and proficiency:

  • Guided practice
  • Realistic simulations
  • Application assessment

DELIVER

Performance Ready

Applied in real work:

  • Supervised application
  • Manager observation and coaching
  • Readiness validation against role expectations

Seven functions, one set of learning dimensions

  • Sales
  • Client Engagement
  • Customer Success
  • Implementation
  • Product
  • Technology
  • Support

Each function has its own learning path with its primary contribution and learning content, and an illustrative DEPLOY exercise, for example "Handle a client escalation and prepare a project status update" for Client Engagement, or "Diagnose a product issue and communicate a resolution" for Support.

Three levels of differentiation

Function-level consistency, role-level relevance and proficiency-level progression. Similar job titles are grouped into role families rather than getting separate curricula.

Individual contributors, specialists, team leads and functional leaders have different development emphasis. Management learning is assigned where responsibilities require it, not compulsory for all senior staff.

Not only for new joiners

People enter at the point that fits them: new joiner, existing employee, internal transfer, promotion, first-time manager or experienced specialist. Nobody repeats what they have already demonstrated.

Employee journeys, page 21

To validate. The proposal marks the seven functional groupings, their boundaries and the future-readiness topics as provisional until SmartWinnr's leadership and subject matter experts confirm them.

Training needs analysis

Five steps from business requirements to prioritised development plans. Each step has a defined output.

  1. Understand business and role requirementsPriorities, responsibilities, KPIs. Consult managers and SMEs.Business and role requirements
  2. Identify required capabilitiesCommon and function-specific knowledge and skills, with observable proficiency.Role-based capability requirements
  3. Assess current capabilitiesKnowledge checks, simulations, manager observation, performance data, self-assessment.Current capability baseline
  4. Validate and analyse gapsCompare required and demonstrated proficiency. Look for non-learning barriers.Validated development needs
  5. Prioritise and planRank by business impact, urgency, role relevance and scale.Prioritised plans

Existing employees and new joiners

One common capability framework, assessed at different points. Existing employees get a baseline at rollout. New joiners are assessed early in onboarding, after learning, and after initial workplace experience. Everyone is reviewed periodically and when something changes.

Principle: evaluate what people can demonstrate, not just what training they have completed.

Kept manageable

  • Start with common and function-specific capabilities; add role detail selectively.
  • One or two assessment methods per competency.
  • Short, structured manager templates.
  • Focus on priority gaps, not every possible need.
  • Pilot in two contrasting functions, refine, then scale.

Three deliverables feed the curriculum: a role-based capability matrix, a prioritised training-needs plan, and team and individual development plans.

Instructional design approach

The same six-stage process for every module. The experience, format and assessment change with the capability being developed.

  1. Define performance objectivesWhat should the employee be able to do?
  2. Identify learning requirementsThe knowledge, skills and behaviours needed.
  3. Structure contentWhat to cover, and in what order.
  4. Design the experienceDemonstrations, exercises, simulations.
  5. Assess and give feedbackCan they apply it? What helps them improve?
  6. Reinforce at workPractice, manager coaching and follow-up.

Performance-led

Start from the workplace performance expected. Every activity serves it.

Practice-centred

Apply knowledge through exercises, scenarios and feedback, not information alone.

Role-relevant

Examples and activities drawn from real job situations.

Adaptive and platform-enabled

Respond to demonstrated needs, without a personalised curriculum for everyone.

Format follows the capability

Capability being developedSuitable formats
Conceptual and product knowledgeShort videos, presentations, digital modules, reading, knowledge checks
Processes and workflowsDemonstrations, screen recordings, walkthroughs, job aids, guided exercises
Communication and interpersonal skillsFacilitated sessions, peer practice, roleplays, AI-based simulations
Problem-solving and decision-makingCase studies, business scenarios, branching exercises
Technical and configuration skillsDemonstrations, sandbox environments, hands-on exercises, practical assessments
Workplace proficiencyObserved task execution, manager feedback, work-output reviews

Using SmartWinnr's own platform. The proposal suggests the organisation experience its product as an internal user: learning paths and tracking for DISCOVER and DECODE, AI roleplays and feedback for DEPLOY, and learning insights for DELIVER. The platform enables learning; it does not replace human feedback or workplace experience. AI simulations are not compulsory for every function.

Worked module: Consultative Selling and Customer Discovery

A Sales module that shows how the approach works end to end, from performance objective to workplace coaching.

Performance objective. Conduct a structured discovery conversation, ask relevant questions, uncover the customer's underlying business challenges, and connect those challenges to an appropriate SmartWinnr solution.

Framework proposed: SPICED. Its five elements map directly onto the performance objective, and also structure the practice scenario and the assessment criteria.

The learning experience in DECODE

Problem-first, framework-guided, practice-led.

  1. Opening challengeWatch a short, imperfect discovery conversation. Spot what the salesperson is missing.Creates the need for a structure
  2. Introduce the frameworkSPICED, with questioning, listening and diagnosis. Contrast weak and strong conversations.
  3. Guided applicationAn evolving fictional banking case. The learner asks questions to uncover each SPICED element.
  4. Independent assessmentA different customer, a large pharmaceutical company, with no SPICED prompts or suggested questions.Tests transfer to a new context

A moment from the banking scenario

Customer context
A fictional bank has invested in employee training but is uncertain whether learning is improving frontline customer interactions.
Learner asks
“How are you currently evaluating whether employees apply what they learn after completing training?”
Customer replies
“We track course completion and assessment scores, but our branch managers still report inconsistent customer conversations.”
Learning feedback
The question has uncovered a gap between training completion and workplace application. Investigate the business impact before recommending a product. Suggested follow-up: “What impact is that inconsistency having on branch performance or customer experience?”

How the learner is assessed

Seven dimensions: the five SPICED elements, conversation skills, and solution alignment without premature pitching. Three observable levels:

Needs developmentMisses important information, asks superficial questions or pitches prematurely.
Demonstrates capabilityConducts structured discovery and uncovers key customer needs.
Strong demonstrationAdapts questions, explores deeper implications and understands the buying context.

No compensation across essential behaviours

A learner cannot make up for a critical weakness in an essential discovery behaviour by scoring well elsewhere. Learners who fall short get targeted feedback, more practice and a chance to reassess. There is no arbitrary limit on attempts.

Extended through DEPLOY

An optional AI-powered scenario practice bank varies industries, personas, objections, stakeholders and complexity, using approved templates with SME oversight. It supports further development and does not replace the core module or its assessment.

Applied through DELIVER

Real customer discovery conversations, with managers observing or reviewing selected interactions, coaching, and readiness validated against role expectations.

To validate. SPICED is a proposed framework, not an assumed SmartWinnr methodology. The banking and pharmaceutical cases are fictional. The assessment rule is a proposal to confirm with Sales leadership.

Rollout and measurement

Parallel preparation with controlled validation and phased deployment. We do not wait for two pilots to finish before preparing the rest of the organisation.

  1. Validate and prepareConfirm the framework, business priorities, role expectations, existing resources and platform capabilities.
  2. Build the foundationDevelop the common framework and DISCOVER. Begin functional paths in parallel, by business need.
  3. Launch and pilotLaunch DISCOVER organisation-wide. Pilot two contrasting functional paths.
  4. Refine and scaleApply pilot findings, complete remaining paths, roll out across all seven functions.

Three connected levels of measurement

Learning

Are employees acquiring the expected knowledge and capabilities? Knowledge checks, practical exercises, roleplay assessments, rubrics.

Workplace application

Are they applying it in real responsibilities? Manager observations, work samples, call and project reviews, coaching records.

Business contribution

Is it contributing to performance? Existing functional KPIs, read alongside other influences.

Measurement leads to decisions

If the evidence showsThen
Employees understand concepts but struggle in practiceImprove guided practice, scenarios or feedback.
They perform well in simulations but struggle at workExamine coaching, processes, tools, workload and workplace conditions.
Capability improves but business indicators do notInvestigate other contributing factors before assuming more training is needed.
Gaps persist across a functionRevisit the needs analysis, role expectations and the intervention.

Design judgement, and what this page claims

The proposal is deliberately honest about its limits.

Choices built into the design

  • Competence over completion: readiness is demonstrated, not attended.
  • Comprehensive in architecture, selective in execution: build what differences in responsibilities justify.
  • Distinguish learning gaps from non-learning barriers such as unclear processes or tools.
  • A gap does not mean repeating the whole programme, only the specific capability.
  • Business indicators are evidence of contribution, not proof that training alone caused an outcome.

What this is, and is not

  • A proposed design built from the assignment brief, public SmartWinnr information and established SaaS practice.
  • Not implemented, so no learner feedback, scores or business results are claimed.
  • Role structures, KPIs, platform capabilities and SPICED are flagged to validate with SmartWinnr stakeholders.
